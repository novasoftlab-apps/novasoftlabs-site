04 / TOOLS

Scanning and security tools

Some tools need particular care:

Device scan and port scanner

Probe devices on your network and check which ports answer. Scanning networks or hosts you do not control may be prohibited by law or by the network's rules. Scan internet hosts only with the owner's permission.

Hidden-camera finder

Looks for devices on the same Wi-Fi network that identify themselves as cameras (ONVIF, RTSP or known camera makers). It cannot detect cameras that are offline, on another network, use mobile data, record locally, or hide their identity. A "nothing found" result does not mean a room is free of cameras. If you believe you are being recorded unlawfully, contact the property owner, the platform you booked through, or the police.

Router security check

Reports likely weaknesses such as weak Wi-Fi encryption, WPS, Telnet, FTP, TR-069, an HTTP-only admin page or UPnP. It is a quick check, not a full security audit, and it does not change any settings. Change router settings only on equipment you manage.

Wake on LAN

Sends a wake-up packet on your local network. Use it only for devices you own or are allowed to manage.

Ping, traceroute, DNS, Whois, outage check and speed test

Contact the hosts and services described in the Privacy Policy. Whois data comes from public registries and may be incomplete, out of date or subject to the registries' own terms of use.

05 / DIAGNOSTICS

Results are informational

Speed, latency, signal, channel, device, port, DNS, route, outage and security results are snapshots from a particular time and setup. They can be affected by device hardware, Android limits (for example, Android hides most MAC addresses and limits Wi-Fi scans), Wi-Fi conditions, guest-network or client isolation, firewalls, provider routing, server load and the permissions you grant. Device names, makers and types are best guesses from what devices announce.

A result is not a guarantee of network performance, security, availability, or the identity or ownership of a device. The app is a diagnostic aid: check important findings with other tools or a qualified professional before making operational, security or purchasing decisions.

06 / BACKGROUND

New-device alerts and background scans

New-device alerts and background scans are optional and off by default. If you turn them on, the app periodically scans the Wi-Fi network you are connected to and may notify you when a new device appears. Android, battery-saving settings and your device maker can delay or stop background work, so alerts may arrive late or not at all. Do not rely on them as a security or intrusion-detection system. You can turn them off at any time in Settings.
