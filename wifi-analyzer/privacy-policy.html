Privacy Policy.

What WiFi Analyzer reads on your phone to run its tools, what it keeps only on your device, which outside services a test contacts, and what Google AdMob processes to show ads.

// At a glance
  • No account, no sign-in, and no analytics SDK. Nova Soft does not run a server that receives your scans, device lists or test results.
  • Scan results, device details, notes, history and speed results are stored only on your phone. They are excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer.
  • Location permission is needed only because Android requires it to show the Wi-Fi name, router BSSID and nearby networks. The app does not read, store or send your GPS location.

  • The app shows full-screen ads from Google AdMob. Google may collect your IP address, advertising ID, device and usage information. Where required by law, you are asked for consent first and can change it any time in Settings > Ad privacy choices.
01 / SCOPE

Who this policy covers

This Privacy Policy applies to the Android app WiFi Analyzer - Network Tool (package com.novasoftapps.networktool), published by Nova Soft ("we," "us," or "our"), and to the Google services built into it. It does not cover the NovaSoft website, the networks or devices you test, or third-party sites and services you open from the app.

Some network information, such as IP addresses, MAC addresses, device names and Wi-Fi identifiers, can be personal data in context, including information about other people's devices on a network. We treat it accordingly and design the app so this information stays on your phone.

Questions about this policy can be sent to support@novasoftlabs.com.

02 / APP DATA

Information the app handles

The information below is read or created on your phone to make the features work. Unless section 06 or 07 says otherwise, it is not sent to Nova Soft or anyone else.

Your connection and Wi-Fi

Network name (SSID), router identifier (BSSID), signal strength, security type, Wi-Fi standard, channel and frequency, link speed, local IPv4 and IPv6 addresses, subnet mask, gateway, DNS servers and MTU, as made available by Android. The Wi-Fi screen also shows your public IP address and its country (see section 06).

Nearby Wi-Fi networks

Names, BSSIDs, channels, signal strengths and security of the networks your phone can see, used for the nearby-networks list, channel graph and best-channel advice.

Devices on your local network

When you run a device scan, the app may learn each device's IP address, host name, MAC address (only when the device announces it), manufacturer (looked up in a vendor table bundled inside the app, with no network request), model, announced services (such as Bonjour, UPnP or casting), open ports and an automatically guessed device type. The phone and router are always listed.

Diagnostic results and what you enter

Results of ping, traceroute, DNS lookup, Whois, port scan, Wake on LAN, Bonjour & UPnP browser, hidden-camera finder, outage check, router security check and speed test, plus the host names, domains, IP addresses and port ranges you type into these tools. You can also add your own device names, types, notes and "trusted" markings.

Settings and history

A history of scans, events and speed results, and app settings such as theme, onboarding status, new-device alerts and background-scan interval.

WiFi Analyzer does not access your contacts, photos, files, microphone, camera, call logs, SMS or accounts, and does not read your precise or approximate location.

Android permissions and why they are needed

Android asks for your approval before the app gets location, nearby Wi-Fi devices or notification access. You can grant or withdraw them at any time in Android settings; denying one only limits the related feature.

04 / PURPOSES

How the information is used

  • To show your current connection, Wi-Fi details, nearby networks and channel advice.
  • To discover and identify devices on your network, remember them between scans and flag new ones.
  • To run the tools you choose and show their results.
  • To keep a local history and create CSV exports when you ask for them.
  • To run background scans and send new-device alerts if you turn them on.
  • To show ads through Google AdMob, which keeps the app free (section 07).

We do not use your network data, device lists or history for advertising, and we do not pass them to the ads SDK.

05 / BACKGROUND

New-device alerts, background scans and the widget

New-device alerts and background scans are off by default. If you turn them on in Settings, the app periodically scans the Wi-Fi network you are connected to (at the interval you choose, from 15 minutes to 6 hours, on Wi-Fi only) and shows a notification when a device it has not seen before appears. Results are stored on your phone like any other scan. You can turn this off at any time in Settings.

The optional home-screen widget shows the number of devices from your latest scan and a Scan button. It reads the same local data and sends nothing elsewhere.

06 / NETWORK TRAFFIC

What happens when you run a test

Network tools work by sending packets or requests. Whoever receives them, and the networks that carry them, can see information that is part of normal network communication, such as your IP address, the time and what was requested. All internet requests made by the app's tools use HTTPS, except DNS and Whois queries, which use their standard unencrypted protocols.

FeatureWho is contacted, and what they receive
Device scan, port scan, hidden-camera finder, router security check, Wake on LAN, Bonjour & UPnP browserDevices on the local network you are connected to, using ICMP, TCP, NetBIOS, mDNS/Bonjour, SSDP/UPnP and ONVIF requests, and your router's DNS server to look up device names. This traffic stays on your local network.
Public IP on the Wi-Fi screenCloudflare (www.cloudflare.com), which returns your public IP address and country. Requested when you open the Wi-Fi details screen or tap Refresh.
Speed testCloudflare (speed.cloudflare.com). Test data is downloaded and uploaded to measure speed; Cloudflare sees your public IP address.
Outage checkGoogle (www.google.com), Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) and Apple (captive.apple.com) connectivity endpoints, plus DNS lookups of google.com, cloudflare.com and wikipedia.org.
Ping, traceroute and DNS lookupThe host you enter and the routers along the way. DNS queries go to your network's DNS server, or to Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Google (8.8.8.8) if it does not answer.
WhoisIANA (whois.iana.org) and the registry servers it refers to, which receive the domain or IP address you enter.
Opening a device's web pageOpens in your browser, which handles it under its own privacy policy.

These services process requests under their own policies, for example Cloudflare, Google, Apple and IANA. Nova Soft does not receive copies of these requests or their results.

Scan only networks and devices you own or are authorized to test. Port scanning and device discovery on networks you do not control may break local rules or laws. See the Terms of Use.

07 / GOOGLE ADMOB

Advertising and Google AdMob

The app is free and shows full-screen (interstitial) ads from Google AdMob, using the Google Mobile Ads SDK. Ads appear at most once every 10 taps, only when you move between screens, and never while a scan, speed test or other tool is running.

What Google may collect

When ads load or are shown, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may automatically collect and share with Google:

  • Device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and app set ID.
  • IP address, which Google may use to estimate your general (approximate) location.
  • App and ad interactions: app launches, ad views, taps and similar activity.
  • Diagnostics: crash logs and performance data related to the ads SDK.
  • Device information: such as device model, operating system version, language and network type.

Google uses this information to deliver, personalise and measure ads, provide ad reporting, and detect and prevent fraud and abuse. Google may also use cookies and similar identifiers for these purposes. Learn more in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and How Google uses cookies in advertising. Google acts under its own Privacy Policy.

Consent and your ad choices

  • EEA, UK and Switzerland: before any ad is requested, the app shows Google's consent form (Google User Messaging Platform). You can consent to personalised ads, or decline. If you decline, Google may still show non-personalised or limited ads, which use data such as your IP address and device information for delivery, frequency capping, measurement and fraud prevention, but not to personalise ads.
  • Change your choice at any time: where a consent form applies, open Settings > Ad privacy choices in the app.
  • US state privacy laws: where Google's consent tools offer an opt-out of personalised ads for your state, it will appear in the same Ad privacy choices option.
  • On any Android device: you can delete or reset your advertising ID in Android settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Settings > Google > Ads), and manage ad personalisation at My Ad Center.

Nova Soft receives only aggregated reports from AdMob, such as the number of ads shown and earnings. We do not receive your advertising ID or individual ad activity, and we do not combine AdMob data with your network data.

08 / GOOGLE PLAY

Ratings through Google Play

The app may show Google Play's in-app rating sheet when you open the app or after a speed test, and Settings has a Rate this app option that opens the Play Store. Google Play decides how often the sheet appears. Any rating or review you give is sent to Google Play under your Google account and Google's policies; the app never receives it or learns whether you rated.

09 / RECIPIENTS

When information is shared

  • Google (AdMob): the advertising-related data described in section 07, collected directly by Google's SDK.
  • Google Play: ratings you choose to submit (section 08).
  • Test destinations: the devices, routers, DNS resolvers, Whois servers and Cloudflare, Google or Apple endpoints involved in a test receive the network traffic needed to perform it (section 06).
  • Your chosen export destination: when you export a CSV file, Android's share sheet sends it to the app, service or person you choose. The export can include device names, IP and MAC addresses, makers and open ports, so share it with care.
  • Legal requirements: we would disclose information we hold only if required by law. Because your app data stays on your phone, we generally have none to disclose.

We do not sell your network data, device lists or diagnostic history, and we do not send them to our own servers.

10 / LIFECYCLE

Retention and deletion

Scans, devices, notes, trust choices, events and speed results stay on your phone until you delete them. To remove them, use Settings > Delete local history. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, removes all app data including settings. App data is excluded from Android cloud backup and device transfer, so it is not copied to a Google account or a new phone.

Exported CSV files are kept wherever you saved or shared them; deleting history in the app does not delete those copies. We cannot delete information kept by a network destination, DNS resolver, speed-test provider or your internet provider as a result of a test.

Google keeps data processed through AdMob according to its own retention policy. You can reset your advertising ID and use Google's controls described in section 07.

11 / CONTROLS

Your rights and choices

  • You decide whether to run a scan or test, turn on alerts or background scans, and export results.
  • You can grant or withdraw Android permissions in your device settings at any time.
  • You can delete local data in Settings or by uninstalling the app.
  • You can change your ad consent in Settings > Ad privacy choices (where offered) and manage your advertising ID in Android settings.

Depending on where you live (for example in the EEA, UK, or certain US states), you may have the right to access, correct, delete or port your personal data, to object to or restrict processing, to withdraw consent, to opt out of targeted advertising, and to complain to your local data protection authority. Because Nova Soft does not hold your app data, most of these rights are exercised directly in the app or with Google. For AdMob, personalised ads rely on your consent where the law requires it, and non-personalised ad delivery and fraud prevention rely on legitimate interests in keeping the app free and secure. Google may process ad data in countries other than your own under its own safeguards.

For any privacy request or question, email support@novasoftlabs.com. We will reply within the time required by applicable law.

12 / CHILDREN

Children's privacy

WiFi Analyzer is a network utility for a general audience and is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age required in your country). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has used the app in a way that resulted in data being collected, contact us and we will help you remove it.

13 / PROTECTION

Security

App data is stored in the app's private storage, protected by Android's app sandbox and your device's encryption and screen lock. The app's internet requests use HTTPS and it does not allow unencrypted HTTP connections, except for protocols that are unencrypted by design (DNS, Whois and local-network discovery). Devices or operators on the network being tested may observe that traffic. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, so protect your phone with a screen lock and share exports with care.
